Swing GG by Week of Month

When a monthly-ATR Golden Gate opens, does the completion rate depend on which week of the month it opened — and on how far price later pulls back toward the pivot?
What a "Swing Golden Gate" is

In Saty Swing mode, the ATR levels are built from the monthly 14-period Wilder ATR and the prior month's close (PMC) — they reset on the 1st and apply all month.

The Golden Gate is the zone from ±38.2% to ±61.8% of that monthly ATR away from PMC. A gate opens at ±38.2% and completes at ±61.8%. The trigger is ±23.6% (call trigger above, put trigger below) and the monthly pivot is the 0-ATR line — the prior month's close itself.

For each month and each direction we find the first day price reaches 38.2%, record which week of the month that is, then ask whether the gate completes by month end — and how far price retraced back toward the pivot before it did. Directions are scored independently; a month can open an upside gate, a downside gate, or both.

Headline

After a Swing gate opens, a pullback toward the pivot means opposite things up vs down — but only while the pullback is shallow. Once price is back at the monthly pivot, the completion edge is gone in both directions.

The two baselines, first. A gate opens when price reaches ±38.2% of the monthly ATR away from the prior-month close, and completes when price reaches ±61.8%. Of the 139 downside gates that opened, 88 completed — a 63% completion rate. Of the 177 upside gates that opened, 102 completed — a 58% completion rate. Every figure below is measured against those two base rates.

A shallow pullback behaves oppositely by direction. Of the downside gates that opened and then bounced back up to the put trigger (−23.6%) before completing, 41 of 53 still completed (77%) — higher than the 63% downside base rate, so a bounce to the put trigger did not hurt. Of the upside gates that opened and then dipped back to the call trigger (+23.6%), only 32 of 61 completed (52%) — below the 58% upside base rate. This shallow-pullback split (it spares puts, it hurts calls) is the finding that survives the daily-bar sensitivity check.

A deeper pullback to the pivot erases the edge both ways. The monthly pivot is the prior-month close (the 0-ATR line). Of downside gates that retraced all the way back to the pivot, 15 of 23 completed (65%) — essentially equal to the 63% downside base rate. Of upside gates that retraced to the pivot, only 6 of 22 completed (27%) — roughly half the 58% upside base rate. And once price crossed the pivot to tag the opposite trigger, completion collapsed: 3 of 32 downside gates (9%) and 3 of 18 upside gates (17%). The pivot is the invalidation line, not a discount.

Separately (Question A below), the completion rate also falls the later in the month a gate first opens — but that is mostly the shrinking number of trading sessions left to travel from 38.2% to 61.8%. Note: the exact retrace percentages above are sensitive to a daily-bar limitation explained in Method & caveats.

Biggest edge · downside

How fast price reaches the gate predicts whether it completes. A violent drop from the put trigger to the gate finishes far more often than a slow grind — the single strongest filter we have found on this setup.

The test. Before a downside gate opens (−38.2%), price must first pass the put trigger (−23.6%). We measured how many trading sessions that traversal took, then asked whether the gate went on to complete (−61.8%) by month end. Of the 139 downside gates, 88 completed — the 63% base rate. It is not just "opened earlier." Fast gates do tend to open earlier in the month (median 18 sessions left vs 12 for slow ones), and the parent study shows earlier opens complete more simply because more days remain. But the speed edge survives holding days-left fixed: among gates with more than 10 sessions left (the reliable group), fast still beat slow 70% (54 of 77) to 63% (20 of 32). And the same pattern shows up — stronger — on the upside gate (fast ≤1 day completes 73% vs 51% slow), independent confirmation that momentum into the gate, not the calendar, is doing the work.

Stacking with weekly context. The other condition that helped was a stretched-up weekly Phase Oscillator (≥61.8) when the downside gate opens — a reversal from a weekly top, which completed 75% (21 of 28). Combining the two: a fast traversal while the weekly PO is extended-up completed 79% (15 of 19), versus 52% (24 of 46) for a slow traversal with a non-stretched weekly. Thin cells (n=19), but consistent with both signals on their own.

How to use it. When a monthly downside Golden Gate opens, check how price got there. A gap or single-session plunge through the put trigger is a momentum tell that favours the gate completing; a multi-day grind into 38.2% is a weaker setup that completes below the base rate. What did not matter: the daily and weekly 21-EMA slope, and price's position relative to the daily 21-EMA, were all within noise (+2 to +3 points). A deeply oversold daily PO (<−61.8) when the gate opens was a small anti-signal (33%, 3 of 9) — the down move was already spent.

Upside vs downside — is it symmetric?

Completion rate, side by side

Green bar = the percentage of opened upside gates that completed; orange bar = the percentage of opened downside gates that completed — one pair per week of the month the gate opened. Compare the retrace ladders above for the sharper asymmetry.
Both directions' completion rates fall as the open slips later in the month. That is partly the clock — a gate opening in week 1 has a median of 19 trading sessions left in the month to travel from 38.2% to 61.8%, versus about 5 sessions for a week-4 open — and partly that a late-month open means price took longer to reach 38.2% in the first place (a slower, less directional move). The cleaner, time-independent signal is the retrace ladder above: a bounce to the same-side trigger is tolerated on the downside but not the upside.

Method & caveats

Data: FirstRateData SPX cash index, daily bars. Levels from the prior month's close + one-month-lagged monthly Wilder ATR(14), matching the lag convention used elsewhere on this site. Warmup leaves 268 months of scored events (2002-01 → 2026-04).

Week of month = calendar week of the open day: days 1–7 = week 1, 8–14 = week 2, 15–21 = week 3, 22–28 = week 4, 29–31 = week 5.

Retrace depth is the deepest bounce back toward the pivot measured from the open day up to (but not including) the completion day — so the retrace genuinely precedes completion. Buckets are nested by depth: same-side trigger (±23.6%) → pivot (0 line) → opposite trigger. "No bounce" = never retraced as far as the same-side trigger.

Open / complete detection uses daily highs and lows, so within a single day the order of a high vs a low is unknown; gates that open and complete the same session are split out separately (no retrace window). Each direction is scored independently per month.

⚠ Daily-bar limit on the retrace ladder (Question B): the retrace window includes the open day, whose intraday high and low may have occurred before the gate opened — so the bucketing is approximate. Excluding the open day from the window reclassifies 74 of the 316 events, and the completion rates in the thin buckets move materially: the downside-pivot bucket's completion rate goes from 65% to 56%, the upside-trigger bucket from 52% to 34%, and the upside-pivot bucket from 27% to 11%. What survives that sensitivity check — and the part you should trust — is the shallow-pullback asymmetry (a bounce to the same-side trigger is tolerated on the downside but lowers the upside completion rate) and the opposite-trigger collapse (under 20% completion either way). The exact pivot and opposite-trigger percentages are noisy on daily data; the definitive test needs intraday (1-minute) bar ordering.

Independent check: re-running this exact analysis on Yahoo Finance ^GSPC daily bars (a different data vendor) over the same window reproduced every completion rate above to within about 1 event per bucket — so the numbers are not an artifact of one data source. This does not address the daily-bar ordering limit above, which applies to any daily OHLC source.

The clock confound: week-of-open and sessions-left-in-month are nearly the same axis. Most of the week-of-month decay is mechanical. The retrace ladder is the time-independent signal.

Caveats: the pivot and opposite-trigger retrace buckets run thin (n<50, flagged faded) — the direction of the effect is robust, the exact percentages are noisy. SPX is the cash index (no overnight gaps inside the month's range the way a tradable gaps), so map onto SPY/ES with care.
